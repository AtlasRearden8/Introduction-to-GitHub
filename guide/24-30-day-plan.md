# Chapter 24: The 30-Day Practice Plan 📅

🎯 **Goals:** Turn what you've read into a habit. This plan gives you one small, doable task a day for 30 days (about 15 to 30 minutes each). By the end you'll have a polished profile, a published site, a collaboration under your belt, and your first open-source contribution.

> 💛 **Be gentle with yourself.** Missed a day? Pick up where you left off. The only way to fall behind is to give up. You can also stretch this plan to 60 days.

---

## 🧰 How to use this plan

1. **Make a repository** called `my-30-days`. Use its README as a **journal**, adding one line a day about what you did and learned.
2. **Check the box** when you finish a day. Copy the list into an issue and tick it there (Chapter 9).
3. **Don't skip ahead.** Each week builds on the last.
4. **Finish with a small celebration.** You earned it.

### 📓 A journal entry looks like this

```markdown
## Day 7
- Did: Created a branch and merged it.
- Learned: Switching branches changes the files on my computer!
- Question: What happens if I delete a branch before merging?
```

## 🌱 Week 1: Foundations

| Day | Task | Chapter |
|-----|------|---------|
| 1 | Create (or tidy) your **GitHub account**. Turn on **2FA** and save your recovery codes. | 2 |
| 2 | Install **GitHub Desktop**, sign in, and check your name and email. | 2 |
| 3 | Create the repository `my-30-days` with a README. Write your Day 1–3 journal entries in the browser. | 3 |
| 4 | Edit a file on github.com and make **three** commits with good messages. Look at the **History** and the **diff**. | 3 |
| 5 | Learn Markdown: make a file with headings, lists, a link, a table, and a callout. Use **Preview**. | 4 |
| 6 | **Clone** `my-30-days` with GitHub Desktop. Edit the README on your computer, commit, and **Push origin**. | 5, 6 |
| 7 | Rest day, or **review**: explain Git vs. GitHub, commit, push, and pull to a friend (or to yourself, out loud). | 1 |

## 🛠️ Week 2: Working safely

| Day | Task | Chapter |
|-----|------|---------|
| 8 | Create a **branch**, make a change, commit, and **publish** it. | 7 |
| 9 | Open your first **pull request** to merge that branch. Read your own **Files changed**. | 8 |
| 10 | **Merge** it, delete the branch, and **Pull origin** on your computer. | 8 |
| 11 | Create **three issues** (tasks you really need to do). Label and assign them. | 9 |
| 12 | Make a **Project board** and add your issues. Move one to **Done**. | 9 |
| 13 | Practice **undoing**: discard an edit, undo a commit, and revert a pushed commit, in a throwaway repo. | 12 |
| 14 | Rest day, or **review**: re-read the "Stuck?" sections you've come across so far. | Various |

## 🤝 Week 3: Together

| Day | Task | Chapter |
|-----|------|---------|
| 15 | Invite a friend (or use a second account) as a **collaborator** on a practice repo. | 10 |
| 16 | Each of you makes a **branch** and opens a **pull request**. | 8, 20 |
| 17 | **Review** each other's pull requests with line comments and one **suggestion**. | 11 |
| 18 | Cause and fix a **merge conflict** on purpose. | 6 |
| 19 | Add a **README**, a **LICENSE**, and a **pull request template** to a repo. | 10, 11, 27 |
| 20 | **Fork** `octocat/Spoon-Knife`, make a change on a branch, and open a pull request (just for practice). | 10 |
| 21 | Rest day, or **review**: explain forks vs. collaborators and when to use each. | 10 |

## 🚀 Week 4: Shine

| Day | Task | Chapter |
|-----|------|---------|
| 22 | Create your **profile README**. Pin two repositories. | 14 |
| 23 | Start your **personal website**: create `YOUR-USERNAME.github.io` and publish a first page. | 13, 19 |
| 24 | Add an About and a Projects page, a theme, and a picture. | 19 |
| 25 | Do the **security checklist**: sessions, email privacy, public/private check. | 16 |
| 26 | Try a **GitHub Action**: the "Say Hello" workflow. | 15 |
| 27 | Find three **"good first issue"** tasks and read their CONTRIBUTING files. | 22 |
| 28 | Make your **first real open-source contribution**, even a one-letter typo fix. | 22 |
| 29 | Share your site with someone. Ask for **feedback** in an issue. | 19 |
| 30 | **Celebrate!** Write a final journal entry: what you built, what you learned, what's next. Open an issue called "Next 30 days" with your next goals. | 24 |

## 🏁 Bonus plan: keep the habit going

You don't need a 30-day rule forever. Aim for small and steady:

| Rhythm | Habit |
|--------|-------|
| **Daily (5 min)** | Commit something. Even a journal line counts. |
| **Weekly** | **Fetch origin**, review your open issues, and tidy a repo. |
| **Monthly** | Run the security checklist (Chapter 16). Update your profile and site. |
| **Quarterly** | Make one open-source contribution. |

## 🧭 Weekly reflection questions

At the end of each week, answer these in your journal:

1. What did I get done?
2. What was confusing?
3. What do I want to try next week?
4. Who could I ask for help?

## 🎯 Pick a goal that matters to you

The plan works best when it's tied to something you care about. Choose one:

- **Get a job or internship:** polish your profile and website, and make two contributions.
- **Write a book:** follow Chapter 21 alongside the plan.
- **Do better at school:** use GitHub for all your notes and group work.
- **Join a community:** contribute to a project you use.
- **Just have fun:** build a recipe collection or a gift for a friend.

Write the goal at the top of your README.

## 🛟 If you get stuck

- **Look at the chapter's "Stuck?" section.** Chapter 29 collects many fixes.
- **Ask for help clearly** (Chapter 17 shows how to ask an AI, and the same recipe works for people): say what you were trying to do, what you clicked, and what you saw.
- **Take a break.** Fresh eyes fix things fast.
- **Find a buddy.** Doing the plan with a friend is far easier.

## ✅ Checkpoint

- [ ] I finished at least 20 of the 30 days (or I'm still going!).
- [ ] My journal has real entries and my profile looks great.
- [ ] I've collaborated with another person.
- [ ] I've made a contribution to a project that isn't mine.
- [ ] I know what I'm doing next.
