# Chapter 14: Your Profile & Portfolio ✨

🎯 **Goals:** Turn your GitHub profile into a friendly front page that shows who you are and what you've made. You'll set up your profile, make a profile README, pin your best projects, and polish a repository so it looks great to visitors.

---

## 🧑 Why your profile matters

Your GitHub profile (`github.com/your-username`) is a public page. People find it when they:

- see your name on a pull request or comment
- look you up before a job interview, class, or collaboration
- follow a link you shared

A thoughtful profile says "I'm learning, I finish things, and I'm easy to work with." You don't need to be an expert. You just need to show up as yourself.

> 🔒 **Only share what you're comfortable with.** Your profile is public. You never have to share your real name, face, location, or employer.

## 🪪 Step 1: Fill in the basics

1. Click your **profile picture** (top right), then **Your profile**.
2. Click **Edit profile**.
3. Add:
   - **Name:** the name you'd like people to see.
   - **Bio:** one friendly line, such as "Learning GitHub 🌱 | Writer | Coffee enthusiast".
   - **Pronouns:** optional.
   - **Company, location, website:** all optional. Skip any you don't want to show.
   - **Social accounts:** link your portfolio or professional page.
4. Click **Save**.

### 📸 Your picture

Click your current picture and choose **Edit → Upload a photo**. A friendly, clear image works well. Many people use an illustration or logo instead of a photo, which is perfectly fine.

### 😀 Your status

Click the small smiley on your profile to set a **status** with an emoji and a short message ("Open to collaborate", "Learning pull requests"). You can set it to clear after a day or a week.

## 🟩 What those green squares mean

On your profile you'll see a grid of squares, the **contribution graph**. Each square is a day. Darker green means more activity: commits, pull requests, issues, and reviews. It's a gentle record of showing up.

- It only counts activity in certain places, such as the main branch of a repository, so a quiet graph doesn't mean you haven't been working.
- You can choose whether to show **private contributions** (activity in private repos) without revealing details. Look for that option in the profile's contribution settings.
- It isn't a score. Take breaks. Nobody is judging.

## 📝 Step 2: Create your profile README

GitHub has a special trick. If you create a **public** repository with the **exact same name as your username**, its README appears at the top of your profile page.

1. Click **+ → New repository**.
2. For **Repository name**, type your exact username. GitHub shows a message: "You found a secret! `username/username` is a special repository."
3. Make it **Public**, tick **Add a README file**, and click **Create repository**.
4. Click the pencil to edit `README.md`.

Here's a friendly starter. Replace the words in capital letters:

````markdown
# Hi there, I'm YOUR NAME 👋

I'm a **WHAT YOU DO** who loves **THINGS YOU LOVE**.

## 🌱 What I'm up to
- Learning how to use GitHub
- Working on **A PROJECT YOU CARE ABOUT**
- Reading **A BOOK OR TOPIC**

## 🛠️ What I like to make
Short list of the things you make: stories, designs, recipes, spreadsheets, lesson plans...

## 📫 Say hello
- Website: [my-site](https://YOUR-USERNAME.github.io)
- Ask me about **TOPIC YOU CAN HELP WITH**

## 💡 Fun fact
Something that makes you smile.
````

5. Click **Commit changes...** and commit.
6. Visit your profile. The README sits right below your photo and bio.

### Ideas for your profile README

- A **short introduction** is better than a long one.
- Add a **picture or banner** from your repo's `images` folder.
- **Link to your best work,** such as your Pages website or a favorite repository.
- Use **headings and emoji** to make it easy to scan.
- Add a **"Currently learning"** list. It shows you're growing.
- Use **collapsible sections** (Chapter 4) for longer details.
- Keep **links up to date.** Check them from time to time.
- **Be careful with add-ons** that pull in charts and badges from other websites. They're fun, but they depend on outside services that can disappear or change.

## 📌 Step 3: Pin your best repositories

**Pinned repositories** appear as cards near the top of your profile. You can pin up to six.

1. On your profile, click **Customize your pins**.
2. Tick the repositories you want to show.
3. Click **Save pins**.

Pin projects that are **finished, tidy, and explained**. A repository with a good README and description makes a strong first impression. Hide the rest in plain sight, since nobody expects every repo to be polished.

## 🏷️ Step 4: Make a repository portfolio-ready

Visitors will judge a project in about ten seconds. Help them.

### ✔️ The checklist

| Item | How |
|------|-----|
| **Clear name** | Settings → General → Repository name. Prefer `recipe-collection` over `project1`. |
| **One-line description** | Click the gear next to **About** on the repo page, and write a short sentence. |
| **Topics** | In the same **About** box, add keywords like `writing`, `design`, `recipes`. They help people find you. |
| **A good README** | See Chapter 4 and the templates in Chapter 27. Say what it is, show a picture, explain how to use it. |
| **A picture** | A screenshot or cover image near the top of the README. |
| **A license** | Add one so others know what they may do (Chapter 10). |
| **A website link** | In the **About** box, tick **Use your GitHub Pages website** if you published one. |
| **Tidy files** | Remove old drafts and stray files, or move them to a folder. |

### 🖼️ A social preview image

When someone shares your repository on social media or in a chat, a **preview image** appears. Set yours:

1. Open the repo, then **Settings → General**.
2. Scroll to **Social preview** and click **Edit → Upload an image**.
3. A picture about 1280 by 640 pixels works well.

## 🏢 Optional: organizations and your profile

If you belong to an **organization** (a team, class, or club), its logo can appear on your profile. In your profile you can choose which organizations to show publicly. It's good to review this setting so you only display what you want.

## 🏅 Achievements

As you use GitHub, you may earn little **achievement badges** on your profile, such as for opening a pull request that gets merged or getting a project starred. They're a fun, optional extra. You can hide them in your profile settings if you'd rather not show them.

## 🌟 Make a "showcase" project

A small project you're proud of is worth more than ten unfinished ones. Pick one thing to polish:

1. Choose a project you like, such as a recipe collection, a poetry portfolio, or a study guide.
2. Write a **great README** with a picture.
3. Add a **Pages website** (Chapter 13).
4. **Pin it** to your profile.
5. Link to it from your profile README.

## 🔒 Privacy check

Before you share your profile widely:

- Look at it while **signed out** (try a private browsing window) to see what strangers see.
- Check your **email settings**: **Settings → Emails**, tick **Keep my email addresses private**.
- Make sure no **private details** are in your README, bio, or commit history.
- Review which **repositories are public** (Chapter 16).

## 🛠️ Try it

1. Write a bio and set a status.
2. Create your profile README and commit it.
3. Pin two repositories.
4. Add a description and two topics to a repository.
5. View your profile in a private window and tweak what you see.

## 🧯 Stuck?

- **My profile README isn't showing:** The repository must be **public**, named exactly like your username, and contain a file called `README.md`.
- **I can't find Customize your pins:** It's on your own profile page, just above the pinned repository cards.
- **My contribution graph is empty:** Activity counts after it's merged or committed to the default branch. It can also take a little while to appear.
- **I mistyped my username in the repo name:** Rename it in **Settings → General → Repository name**.

- [ ] I've pinned my best projects.
- [ ] One repository has a description, topics, and a good README.
- [ ] I checked my profile as a stranger would see it.
